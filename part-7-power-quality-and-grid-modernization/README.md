# Part VII — Power Quality and Grid Modernization

Parts IV to VI built the power system of the twentieth century: large synchronous generators, transmission, one-way distribution to passive customers, and protection. This part deals with what has changed. Customers' loads are now dominated by power electronics that distort current and are sensitive to brief disturbances; generation is moving to inverter-connected wind, solar and storage, much of it on distribution feeders and behind customers' meters; the network is wrapped in sensors, communication links and software that let it observe and control itself; and energy use itself has become something to be measured, managed and reduced. The common thread is that delivering energy is no longer enough. The quality of the voltage waveform, the behaviour of inverters in the first second after a fault, the flexibility of demand and the efficiency of use are now engineering quantities with limits, standards, measurements and costs of their own.

## By the end of this part you should understand

- How supply voltage departs from the ideal balanced sinusoid (magnitude and frequency variation, harmonics, flicker, transients, sags and swells, unbalance) and what limits IEC, IEEE and EN standards set on each.
- How harmonic voltages, sag depths, flicker and unbalance are estimated from fault levels and load data, measured to IEC 61000-4-30, and mitigated, including the harmonic resonance of capacitor banks.
- How smart meters, grid communication networks, substation and distribution automation, state estimation and synchrophasors give an operator visibility and control down to the customer.
- How distributed resources, microgrids and demand response add flexibility, how energy management systems from the control centre to the building close the loop, and what cybersecurity and resilience demand of them.
- How grid-connected inverters synchronise to the grid, control active and reactive power, and meet grid codes for ride-through, frequency and voltage support and anti-islanding.
- Why falling inertia and system strength limit the instantaneous share of inverter-based generation, and how storage, forecasting, grid-forming control and synchronous condensers push that limit back.
- How energy is audited and managed in plants and buildings, how motor efficiency, power-factor correction and loss reduction are evaluated, and how life-cycle cost decides between options.

## How this part connects to the rest of the book

Part VII stands on almost everything before it. Harmonic resonance and sag calculations use the fault levels and network models of Chapters 17 and 19; frequency support and penetration limits rest on the stability theory of Chapter 20; voltage rise from distributed generation and feeder automation extend Chapter 22; ride-through and inverter fault current return to the protection of Chapter 23; switching transients connect to the surge and insulation coordination of Chapter 24. The machines of Part II are what harmonics heat and unbalance derates, the drives of Part III are the main harmonic sources, and the plants and batteries of Chapters 15 and 16 are the resources whose grid behaviour Chapter 27 specifies. Part VIII takes the same installations into safety and design practice.

Among the sibling books, the circuit and signal theory comes from *Circuits, Signals and Communications*: AC power, power factor and harmonics ([Ch. 9](https://github.com/The-Studio-of-Sadiq/Circuits-Signals-and-Communications/blob/main/part-2-transient-and-ac-analysis/09-ac-power.md)), three-phase circuits and symmetrical components ([Ch. 10](https://github.com/The-Studio-of-Sadiq/Circuits-Signals-and-Communications/blob/main/part-2-transient-and-ac-analysis/10-three-phase-circuits.md)), resonance and switching transients ([Ch. 7](https://github.com/The-Studio-of-Sadiq/Circuits-Signals-and-Communications/blob/main/part-2-transient-and-ac-analysis/07-second-order-circuits.md)), the DFT used by power-quality instruments ([Ch. 22](https://github.com/The-Studio-of-Sadiq/Circuits-Signals-and-Communications/blob/main/part-5-digital-signal-processing/22-discrete-fourier-transform.md)), the communication theory beneath grid networks ([Part VI](https://github.com/The-Studio-of-Sadiq/Circuits-Signals-and-Communications/blob/main/part-6-communication-theory/25-communication-system-fundamentals.md)) and the feedback theory beneath PLLs, droop and AGC ([Part VII](https://github.com/The-Studio-of-Sadiq/Circuits-Signals-and-Communications/blob/main/part-7-control-system-fundamentals/34-feedback-and-stability.md)).
