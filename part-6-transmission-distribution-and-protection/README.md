# Part VI — Transmission, Distribution, and Protection

Part V treated the power system as a network of per-unit impedances. Part VI looks at the hardware those impedances stand for and the equipment that keeps it safe. A transmission line is a set of conductors on towers whose geometry fixes its reactance, charging and corona. A distribution feeder is a radial chain of cables, transformers and loads whose end voltage sags every evening and, increasingly, rises at noon when rooftop solar exports. Protection is the system of instrument transformers, relays, breakers and fuses that must find a fault within tens of milliseconds and remove only the faulted part. High-voltage engineering is the discipline that keeps insulation intact against lightning, switching surges and decades of electrical stress. One idea links the four chapters: every component must carry normal power efficiently and survive its abnormal duty (fault current, overvoltage, lightning) without endangering people or the network.

## By the end of this part you should understand

- How the resistance, inductance and capacitance of overhead lines and cables follow from conductors, bundles and tower geometry, and which line model is accurate enough for a given length.
- Why long AC lines are limited by voltage regulation, the Ferranti effect and stability rather than by conductor heating, and how shunt reactors, series capacitors, SVCs and STATCOMs relax those limits.
- When high-voltage DC is the better choice, and how line-commutated and voltage-source converters differ in what they demand from the AC system.
- How radial and ring distribution networks are arranged, how feeder voltage drop and losses are calculated, and how tap changers, regulators and distributed generation change the voltage profile.
- How overcurrent, differential, distance and earth-fault protection detect faults, and how relays, fuses and breakers are coordinated so that protection is both selective and sensitive.
- How insulation is coordinated against lightning and switching surges, how surge arresters and earthing work, and how high-voltage equipment is tested and worked on safely.

## How this part connects to the rest of the book

Part VI uses the analytical tools of Part V throughout. The per-unit models and power-flow solutions of Chapters 17 and 18 describe the networks whose lines and feeders are designed here. The fault currents of Chapter 19 are the duties that breakers, cables and earthing systems must survive and the signals that protection relays measure. The stability limits of Chapter 20 are what make long-line compensation and fast protection worth their cost. The transformers of Chapter 4 reappear as distribution and instrument transformers. Looking forward, the voltage and harmonic problems that distribution passes to customers are taken up in Chapter 25 (Power Quality); the automation and distributed resources that are changing distribution and protection in Chapters 26 and 27; and the hazards to people from faults, arcs and touch voltages in Chapters 29 and 30.

Two sibling books supply the foundations this part builds on. *Circuits, Signals and Communications* (Book 3) owns the wave theory of lines, including the telegrapher's equations, surge impedance, reflection and the exact ABCD constants of a long line ([Ch. 14](https://github.com/The-Studio-of-Sadiq/Circuits-Signals-and-Communications/blob/main/part-3-advanced-network-theory/14-transmission-lines.md)), the two-port and nominal-π line models ([Ch. 12](https://github.com/The-Studio-of-Sadiq/Circuits-Signals-and-Communications/blob/main/part-3-advanced-network-theory/12-two-port-networks.md)), and the three-phase circuit theory and symmetrical components that protection relies on ([Ch. 10](https://github.com/The-Studio-of-Sadiq/Circuits-Signals-and-Communications/blob/main/part-2-transient-and-ac-analysis/10-three-phase-circuits.md)). *Engineering Physics: Top Down* owns the electromagnetic physics: the field definitions of inductance and capacitance ([Ch. 11](https://github.com/The-Studio-of-Sadiq/Engineering-Physics/blob/main/CHAPTERS/CHAPTER%2011.md)), electromagnetic waves and their propagation, which underlie lightning and switching surges ([Ch. 12](https://github.com/The-Studio-of-Sadiq/Engineering-Physics/blob/main/CHAPTERS/CHAPTER%2012.md)), and the transport and heat-transfer laws that set conductor resistance and thermal ratings ([Ch. 13](https://github.com/The-Studio-of-Sadiq/Engineering-Physics/blob/main/CHAPTERS/CHAPTER%2013.md) and [Ch. 19](https://github.com/The-Studio-of-Sadiq/Engineering-Physics/blob/main/CHAPTERS/CHAPTER%2019.md)). This part takes those results as given.
